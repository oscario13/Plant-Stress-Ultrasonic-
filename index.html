Can you hear a plant scream?

Not metaphorically. Stressed plants emit real sound — short ultrasonic pops caused by their own internal plumbing failing under pressure. Here's the mechanism, how researchers proved it, and the actual recordings.

Why plants pop

Water moves up a stem under tension, pulled by evaporation at the leaves — the same physics that lets you suck water up a straw. Drought pushes that tension past a breaking point: air gets pulled into a water channel, the column snaps from liquid to a gas-filled void, and that snap releases a burst of sound (cavitation). Cutting a stem causes the same failure faster, by exposing the tensioned water straight to open air.
